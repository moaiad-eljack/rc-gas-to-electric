01

The project

A nitro RC car runs on a tiny gas engine. We are taking that engine out and putting in a brushless motor, an ESC and a LiPo battery.

Before touching the car we wanted to understand the electric side for real. So the first phase was all theory: what each part does, how they connect, and what kills them.

All four of us work on every part together. Nobody owns one piece of the car, so the electrical and mechanical sides end up learning from each other the whole way.

02

What we did, step by step

Step 1

Motors

Our first meeting. We kept it basic on purpose. We didn't want to memorize part numbers, we wanted to know why a motor spins at all and what really changes between brushed and brushless.

  • We are going brushless. More power, runs cooler, almost nothing wears out.
  • kV times voltage sets how fast the motor wants to spin, so motor and battery get picked as a pair.
Read the motor notes
Step 2

The ESC

Second meeting. Once the motor made sense, the next question was what tells it how fast to go. That's the ESC, the box between the battery and the motor.

  • The ESC decides how much of the battery actually reaches the motor.
  • Its built in BEC also powers the receiver and the steering servo, so we don't need a second battery.
Read the ESC notes
Step 3

Wiring

After the ESC we looked up how everything plugs together. We saved a few diagrams we found online, then sketched our own version so we understood it and weren't just copying one.

  • Polarity is not optional. Red to red, black to black. Backwards kills the ESC on the spot.
  • The battery goes in last and comes out first.
Read the wiring notes
Step 4

Batteries

The LiPo is the one part of this car that can actually hurt somebody. So we turned the rules into a cheat sheet we can look at before anyone charges anything.

  • Never below 3.0 V per cell, never above 4.2 V.
  • Balance charger only, in a LiPo bag, and never left alone.
Read the battery notes
Step 5

Writing it down

Then we set up a GitHub repo and a shared doc, went back through our notes, and fixed the parts we had wrong. Notes now go in after every meeting, not weeks later.

  • Every phase gets its own folder: what we did, what we learned, what it leads to.
  • We don't write ahead. The next meeting gets one line and that's it.

03

What we got wrong

Our first notes had mistakes in them. We left the record in.

We wrote

Brushed motors work with magnets touching the shaft.

What's true

The magnets are fixed to the can. The brushes are what touch the spinning part.

We wrote

Brushed has 3 wires, brushless has 2.

What's true

Other way around. Brushed has 2, brushless has 3.

Both are fixed in the notebook, with the correction written right next to them. If something in there is wrong, it's because we were wrong at the time, and we fix it once we figure it out.
